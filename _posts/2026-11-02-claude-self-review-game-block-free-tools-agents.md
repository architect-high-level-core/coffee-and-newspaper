A new week starts with a lot of AI news from the weekend: Claude's new self-check, a block on rebuilding video games, a full animation studio made of free tools, a private dictation app, how to build AI agents you can rely on, and the money behind the AI boom. Here are six stories, plus one surprise.

**How we checked:** the stories come from the public news summaries on X from the last 36 to 48 hours. These summaries are written by an AI from many posts; we did not check them against original pages, so facts from them are labelled "according to an X news summary". We did not test any product.

**Key terms:**

- **AI agent:** an AI program that completes a task on its own in several steps, using tools along the way.
- **Open source:** the program code is public and may be used and changed for free.
- **On-device:** the AI runs directly on your own computer; nothing is sent to the internet.
- **Harness:** the framework around an AI model: tools, memory, check steps and approvals, so the agent works reliably.
- **Capex:** capital spending, here mostly money for data centers and chips.

## 1. Claude now checks itself, and many users find that creepy

- **What's new:** according to an X news summary, Anthropic introduced a feature at the end of October in which Claude writes a short internal safety report after every answer. In it, Claude flags possible breaches of the usage rules.
- **Why Anthropic does it:** the system is meant to find misuse that older filters missed, such as accounts that secretly try to "tap" Claude's abilities. According to the summary, thousands of such accounts were banned.
- **The criticism:** many users call it a "digital informant", because the reports are collected and can be reviewed by people. Developers, for example in IT security, complain that it gets in the way of their legitimate work.

## 2. Claude no longer rebuilds known games, and overshoots

- **What happened:** according to an X news summary, around October 27 Anthropic added a block: Claude should no longer rebuild certain existing video games, not even under a new name. The reason is copyright, as more and more AI clones show up on platforms like Steam.
- **The problem:** users say the filter also blocks harmless things, such as mods for their own project, crash analyses, or even a public-domain board game from 1904.
- **Where it stands:** Anthropic's Addy Osmani confirmed the change and said the filter is being refined based on user feedback.

## 3. A whole animation studio made of free tools

- **The idea:** according to an X news summary, designers are building complete studios for motion graphics (animations, explainer videos) out of free programs from GitHub, controlled by AI.
- **The building blocks:** Remotion creates videos from program code, Three.js makes 3D in the browser, and FFmpeg exports the finished files. Add tools for voice-over and subtitles.
- **Why it matters:** such a setup is said to replace expensive programs like After Effects at almost no cost. Creators share lists of 10 to 22 tools and examples that are said to look agency-grade.

Original article: [X News: Creators Build Free Motion Studios with Open-Source AI Tools](https://x.com/i/trending/2117023504214208622)

## 4. Hex: dictation without your voice leaving the house

- **What it is:** according to an X news summary, Hex is a free dictation app from the OpenCode team for Mac, Windows and Linux. You hold a key, speak, and the text lands straight in the program that is open.
- **The clever part:** everything runs on-device, so no cloud and no subscription. Users praise it as faster than paid services like Wispr Flow.
- **What people use it for:** mostly to speak AI instructions or coding tasks quickly instead of typing them.

Original article: [X News: Hex Voice Dictation App Wins Developers with Speed and Privacy](https://x.com/i/trending/2116678391482953816)

## 5. Reliable AI agents: fewer magic words, more framework

- **Free course:** according to an X news summary, Anthropic developers published a free course on building agents with Claude Opus 5.5 that plan, act, remember and check their own work.
- **The trend behind it:** instead of polishing the prompt, developers build a "harness" around the model: tools, memory, check steps and human approval before important actions. Examples are nightly self-reviews or a rule that code only goes live after approval.
- **Tips from the community:** a job description as the agent's basic instruction, a project file (CLAUDE.md) with fixed rules, and a small local memory so you don't have to explain everything again each time. The 40 percent time saving mentioned comes from user reports, not from an independent study.

## 6. The money behind the AI boom: bubble or just tight money?

- **The debate:** according to an X news summary, investor Raoul Pal pushes back on the idea that the AI boom is over. He explains the recent drop in share prices with tight money in the market (after an oil price shock from the Iran war), not with falling demand.
- **The numbers mentioned:** Microsoft's cloud Azure grew 48 percent to 33.2 billion dollars; capex of 45 billion dollars came in below plan because of delivery delays. Anthropic is said to be on a 46 billion dollar annual revenue pace.
- **The other side:** skeptics point, for example, to a smaller OpenAI funding round of 11 billion dollars. All numbers come from the X summary and were not checked against original reports.

Original article: [X News: Raoul Pal Defends AI Investment Boom Amid Selloff Debate](https://x.com/i/trending/2117099344809648171)

## Surprise of the day: AI companies ask religions about morality

- **What's happening:** according to an X news summary, people from AI companies like Anthropic are meeting clergy and scholars from more than 15 faiths. The goal is to give chatbots a better sense of right and wrong.
- **Why:** Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah explains that the ethical questions of AI are the same ones religions have thought about for thousands of years. Online, people argue fiercely about which tradition should set the tone.
