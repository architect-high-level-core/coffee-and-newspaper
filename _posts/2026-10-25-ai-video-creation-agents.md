For a long time, "AI video" meant one thing: type a prompt, get a five- to ten-second clip. To make an actual film you had to generate dozens of clips, describe your characters again and again, and stitch everything together yourself in an editing program.

In autumn 2026 a new category is taking shape: **film agents**. You type a sentence or paste a script, and the agent writes the screenplay, breaks it into scenes and shots, casts the characters once, renders every shot with dialogue and sound, and assembles a finished cut. This article looks at seven of them, plus a few honourable mentions, and closes with a closer look at **Stanley**, a creator agent that keeps coming up in this context.

**How we checked:** we read the official product and help pages, Forward Future's AI tools directory, Forward Future's newsletter of October 23, 2026 and one Reddit discussion. We did not test the tools. Prices are the vendors' own figures in US dollars as of October 24, 2026; marketing claims are labelled as such.

**Key terms:**

- **AI agent:** an AI program that does not just answer, but completes a task on its own in several steps, using tools along the way.
- **Agentic (for video):** the tool plans by itself (script, scenes, shots) and then generates several shots in a row, instead of one clip per button press.
- **Text-to-video / script-to-video:** a written prompt, or a whole script, is turned into video.
- **Shot:** one continuous piece of footage, such as a close-up. A short film consists of many shots.
- **Storyboard:** a sequence of sketches or stills showing each shot before it is filmed.
- **Consistency:** the same character looks the same in every scene (face, clothes, room). This has been the hardest problem in AI video.
- **Credits:** prepaid units many AI services use for billing; every generation uses some.
- **Alpha:** a very early test version.
- **UGC (user-generated content):** content that looks as if an ordinary user filmed it on a phone, for example a product testimonial.
- **Microdrama / vertical drama:** very short serial episodes in portrait format for phones.

## 1. Creating, not cutting

We deliberately left out tools that only edit existing footage. Two examples from Forward Future's directory show the difference: **Alex's DaVinci Editing Skills** are free, MIT-licensed instructions that let an AI agent clean dialogue and make a first cut in DaVinci Resolve, and **Klap** turns long videos into short clips. Both are useful, but neither creates new footage.

The community is sober about what generation can do. In a Reddit thread on r/artificial about making a four-minute short film with AI, users explain that you still have to generate and assemble many short pieces; one commenter (February 2026) warns that tools sometimes change prices or drop features in the middle of a project and that post-processing time should be budgeted. These are individual opinions.

## 2. Mago: from an idea to a finished cut (new, alpha)

**What it does:** Mago started as a tool that restyles footage you already have. On October 9, 2026 it announced that it can now start from nothing. You describe an idea (the agent asks a few questions and writes the script with you) or paste a script. Mago splits the story into up to 30 shots, creates a reference image for every character, location and prop (characters also get a multi-view sheet), and renders each shot with those references attached. Dialogue written as "SPEAKER: line" produces voices, sound effects and music in the same render. The pinned versions play in order and download as one MP4.

**Agentic?** Clearly yes; Mago says it "works through every step on its own". You can swap any single shot, and only changed shots are re-rendered.

**Price:** the site invites you to "start generating free"; we found no specific prices on the pages we read. The "Full AI drama production" mode is explicitly in alpha.

Original articles: [Mago blog, Oct 9, 2026](https://www.mago.studio/blog/mago-can-now-make-a-film-from-a-script) · [Mago: From idea to video](https://www.mago.studio/generate)

## 3. Pixel Dojo Film Studio: one sentence in, check the plan for free

**What it does:** you type an idea and choose micro-drama, short film or trailer, vertical or wide. Film Studio writes it as short scenes with every shot, line and sound, which you can edit before paying. Each character then gets a portrait you can swap for your own character or photo. Every scene is filmed with spoken dialogue and sound and cut together with titles and captions. An advanced mode adds a storyboard frame per shot and a choice of 11 video models.

**Agentic?** Yes: planning, casting, generation and editing run as one workflow. Export as 720p MP4 (16:9 or 9:16) or as individual clips plus the script for Premiere, Resolve or CapCut.

**Price:** included in every paid plan from $10 per month, according to the vendor. Planning costs no credits; nothing is charged until you approve the plan.

Original article: [Pixel Dojo: AI film maker](https://pixeldojo.ai/ai-film-maker)

## 4. Pixo: a "director agent" for multi-scene stories

**What it does:** Pixo turns a story idea, outline or short-film script into a connected short film with storyboard, consistent characters, voice, sound and a final edit. Its agent plans the storyboard, generates the scenes with leading video models and refines the whole video in one workspace. Pixo says output is 1080p with commercial rights.

**Agentic?** Yes; Pixo explicitly calls it a "director agent" that writes the script and generates the video.

**Price:** 200 free credits to start, no card needed, according to Pixo.

Original article: [Pixo: AI Short Film Maker](https://pixo.video/use-cases/ai-short-film-maker)

## 5. Okay Create: script first

**What it does:** Okay Create's motto is that "movies don't start with prompts. They start with scripts." You write the screenplay yourself or let the "Assistant Director" draft it from a single idea; it is formatted as a production blueprint and AI agents take it from there, shot by shot. The Assistant Director asks questions if you have no idea yet, builds actors (age, look, personality), offers headshots to choose from and "films on command". You can also bring your own footage.

**Agentic?** Yes, with a focus on narrative short films, features, animation and sketches. Unusually, the site itself recommends other tools for quick clips or commercial content.

**Price:** a single scene of about 15 seconds can be tried for free; sign-up needs no credit card. Prices for full films were not shown on the page we read.

Original article: [Okay Create](https://okaycreate.com/)

## 6. LTX Studio: the professional workshop with an open model

**What it does:** LTX Studio is a web platform for filmmakers, ad agencies and creative teams. You can upload a script to generate scenes and storyboards, or start from a concept, an image or a video. It adds camera control, keyframes, style presets, a timeline editor and sound design. "Elements" keep AI characters, objects and locations consistent across a project. The underlying LTX-2 model is fully open source according to the company; the current version is LTX 2.5.

**Agentic?** Partly. LTX plans scenes and storyboards from a script but emphasises control by the user over a fully autonomous agent.

**Price:** free and paid plans, according to its FAQ.

Original article: [LTX Studio](https://ltx.studio/)

## 7. HeyGen Video Agent: explainer and business videos from a prompt

**What it does:** HeyGen is known for talking avatars. Its Video Agent turns a prompt into a finished video: you chat with it, it researches, writes a video plan and script, then generates avatar scenes, AI images, AI video clips and motion graphics. Changes are requested in chat ("change the third scene"), or you edit a copy manually.

**Agentic?** Yes: plan first, then generate and assemble several assets. The focus is on explainers, marketing and business content rather than fiction.

**Price (HeyGen help centre):** chatting, planning and editing the plan are free. Credits are used only when generation starts: about 80 credits per minute in Standard mode and about 240 in Seedance mode, with an estimate shown before generation. Via the API, Prompt to Video costs $0.0333 per second, about $2 per finished minute.

Original articles: [HeyGen Video Agent FAQ](https://help.heygen.com/en/articles/16007192-video-agent-faq) · [HeyGen credits](https://help.heygen.com/en/articles/15126059) · [HeyGen API pricing](https://developers.heygen.com/docs/pricing)

## 8. For ads: Genrok (and Reloop)

**What Genrok does:** paste a product link or describe what you need in one message. According to the vendor, the agent reads the product page, picks the angle, script, scene and format, and delivers UGC-style video ads and static ads in 87 languages.

**Agentic?** Yes; Genrok calls itself an "autonomous agent": one message in, a finished campaign out. Claims such as "102% realism" are marketing and could not be verified. Genrok itself notes that a human creator brings an audience and real product experience that the tool does not replace.

**Price (vendor):** start free, then pay as you go at $1 = 8 credits with top-ups from $10. UGC video costs 6 to 22 credits per second; a 15-second 480p video starts at 90 credits (about $11 at pay-as-you-go rates). Monthly plans range from $100 to $2,000.

**Similar:** Reloop describes itself as an "AI creative director". From a product link it writes hooks and scripts, picks a talking avatar, generates product visuals, B-roll and music, and assembles a finished, subtitled ad, plus variations for A/B testing. We did not check Reloop's prices.

Original articles: [Genrok AI](https://genrok.ai/) · [Reloop AI Ad Agent](https://reloop.so/features/ai-agent/)

## 9. Also worth a look

- **ngram:** listed in Forward Future's directory as an "agentic AI video creation platform" that turns documents, PDFs, URLs or screen recordings into product and explainer videos: it researches, storyboards, writes scripts, generates voiceovers and edits. Freemium according to the listing ([Forward Future](https://forwardfuture.com/tools/tag/ai-video-generator-tools)).
- **Pexo:** says it turns a line, a script or a photo into a 5- to 120-second short film, combining several video models (including Kling and Seedance) at up to 4K. Vendor claims only; prices not checked ([Pexo](https://pexo.ai/create/short-film)).

## 10. Surprise from the news: Swedish beats Hindi

Forward Future's team tested how well the newest frontier models translate seven languages. Hindi has about 600 million speakers, Swedish about 10 million, yet Swedish received some of the best translations and Hindi was among the worst. Across all seven languages, the number of speakers had almost nothing to do with quality. For anyone localising AI-generated videos, that is a reason to have every language checked by a native speaker.

Original article: [Forward Future newsletter, Oct 23, 2026](https://forwardfuture.com/newsletter/daily/2026-10-23/ai-agents-map-brain-disease-hackers-weaponize-ai-meta-s-stock-plan)

## 11. What Stanley actually does

Stanley appeared in our October 23 article on the Personal Agents Market Map ("The Personal Agents Market Map Explained") in the "Creators" section. Because it is often mentioned alongside content tools, we re-read the official pages.

- **Who makes it:** Stan, the company behind Stan Store, a platform where creators sell digital products. Stan calls Stanley an "AI Head of Content". Stanley and Stan Store are separate products with separate subscriptions.
- **What it does:** Stanley connects to your LinkedIn, Instagram and X accounts (more are planned), learns your voice from past posts and voice notes, and analyses which content performs in your niche and why. It then drafts post ideas, hooks, captions, carousels and reel scripts, builds a weekly content plan and nudges you to stay consistent.
- **How you use it:** like a colleague you text, via iMessage, Telegram or the web. According to the site it also reaches out proactively with ideas. A typical loop shown there: you send a voice note, Stanley drafts posts for X and LinkedIn, suggests a time, publishes after your approval and later reports the numbers.
- **A small contradiction:** the website shows Stanley scheduling and posting, while Stan's blog says it will "never post anything on your behalf without your oversight". Read together: it posts what you have approved.
- **Is it a video agent?** No. Stanley does not generate video. It writes scripts and plans content, but you still film yourself. It is a strategy and writing agent for social media. Neither page we read states a price.

Original articles: [Stan: Meet Stanley, Your AI Head of Content (updated Sep 16, 2026)](https://stan.store/blog/stanley/) · [Stanley official site](https://www.getstanley.ai/welcome)

## 12. Takeaways

- The real step forward in 2026 is not a better single clip but agents that run the whole pipeline: script, casting, many shots and the edit, with characters that stay consistent.
- It is not yet a "make me a feature film" button. Most tools target seconds to a few minutes; Mago mentions up to 30 shots per run, and its script-to-film mode is still alpha.
- Several tools let you review the plan for free before paying (Pixel Dojo, HeyGen) or offer free starts (Pixo, Okay Create, Genrok), which makes low-risk testing easy.
- AI ads designed to look like real customer videos raise trust questions. Labelling AI content clearly and checking each ad platform's rules is the safe route.
