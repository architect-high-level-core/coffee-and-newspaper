Today brings a mixed look at the AI and tech news of the last 24 hours: voice AI that hears feelings, Google's Gemini 4 Argon working inside Blender, agent teams instead of single agents, a free hub for AI coding helpers, a phone with no apps, the fight over "vibe coding" and the drop in AI stocks. Here are seven stories, plus one surprise.

**How we checked:** the stories come from the public news summaries on X from the last 24 hours. These summaries are written by an AI from many posts; we did not check them against original pages, so facts from them are labelled "according to an X news summary". We did not test any product.

**Key terms:**

- **AI agent:** an AI program that completes a task on its own in several steps, using tools along the way.
- **API:** an interface that lets one program use the services of another program.
- **Open source:** the program code is public and may be used and changed for free.
- **Vibe coding:** you describe in plain words what an app should do and let an AI write the code, without checking it closely yourself.
- **Capex:** capital spending, here mostly money for data centers and chips.

## 1. Voice AI now hears feelings and answers almost without a pause

- **What's new:** according to an X news summary, Hume AI introduced an "Audio Decision API". It listens to audio directly, without turning it into text first, and picks up tone of voice, emotions, laughter and how many people are speaking.
- **How good it is said to be:** it was trained on more than 10 million human ratings and, according to the company, agrees with people in 70.7 percent of cases.
- **Related:** Chatbase launched "Voice 2.0". Its model "Dessa" is meant to notice when you have finished speaking, so talks with voice bots run without awkward pauses.

Original article: [X News: Voice AI Advances with Emotion Detection and Fast Responses](https://x.com/i/trending/2117358114655625645)

## 2. Gemini 4 Argon runs Blender and builds a retro video

- **What happened:** according to an X news summary, a Google team had its new model Gemini 4 Argon create a complete retro video, with a 3D intro animation, a drawn cat and its own music.
- **How it worked:** the AI made the images in the 3D program Blender and the sound in the program SuperCollider by writing scripts itself, through Google's Antigravity platform. The result was played from a Raspberry Pi on an old tube TV.
- **Why it matters:** the example shows that AI models can now do multi-step work inside real professional software. Skeptics point out how much computing power is behind it.

Original article: [X News: Google's Gemini 4 Argon Generates Retro Video on Vintage CRT](https://x.com/i/trending/2117383438722613646)

## 3. From a single agent to a whole agent team

- **The trend:** according to an X news summary, developers are moving from one AI agent to systems of many agents that plan, check each other and work with little supervision.
- **Who is behind it:** Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch calls for controlling models, data and runtime yourself through open source. Andrew Ng released a two-hour course on the topic, and Google published its own training.
- **The warning:** according to the summary, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang says people who ignore agent teams risk seeing their role change a lot. Entry-level jobs in finance and IT would be hit first.

Original articles: [X News: Developers Push AI from Single Agents to Scalable Systems](https://x.com/i/trending/2117260407081779344) · [X News: Harness Engineering Transforms AI Agents from Prompts to Systems](https://x.com/i/trending/2116680654171631672)

## 4. T3 Code: a free hub for AI coding helpers

- **What it is:** according to an X news summary, T3 Code is a free app by Theo Browne for computer, browser and phone. Different coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex and Grok Build run inside one interface.
- **Cost:** the app itself is open source (MIT license) and free; you bring your own subscriptions for the individual AI services.
- **The criticism:** some users report bugs, and Theo openly admits that not everything runs smoothly yet. Fans praise that it works well on the go, even with a weak connection.

Original article: [X News: T3 Code AI App Faces Praise and Criticism Online](https://x.com/i/trending/2117018208372744409)

## 5. Cyclon: a phone that just listens instead of using apps

- **The idea:** according to an X news summary, the two-person startup Cyclon (from the Y Combinator startup program) plans a smartphone whose operating system is the AI itself. You say what you want, and the AI gets it done across different apps, or builds a new one when needed.
- **Privacy:** the data is meant to stay on the device.
- **Where it stands:** pre-orders are open with a 199-dollar deposit for a prototype based on a Google Pixel 8a; its own hardware is announced for the second quarter of 2027. Critics ask about battery life and whether such a small team can pull it off.

Original article: [X News: Cyclon Unveils AI-Powered Phone to Replace iPhone](https://x.com/i/trending/2117459285068173454)

## 6. Vibe coding: built fast, but also the wrong thing built fast

- **What it's about:** according to an X news summary, developers are arguing about "vibe coding", letting an AI build apps from a description, for example with Cursor, Claude Code or Antigravity, without checking the code.
- **For and against:** for quick prototypes it is great. Critics warn about problems with maintenance, security and understanding. One sharp line from the debate: it makes it easier than ever to build the wrong product.
- **A number:** according to the Stack Overflow 2026 survey cited in the summary, 65.9 percent of developers use AI assistants, but most check the results carefully.

Original article: [X News: Vibe Coding Splits Developers on AI Tools](https://x.com/i/trending/2117262249714921592)

## 7. AI stocks under pressure: 2 trillion dollars gone in October

- **What happened:** according to an X news summary, the Nasdaq 100 tech index lost more than 2 trillion dollars in market value in October, because investors doubt the huge spending on AI.
- **The examples named:** Alphabet reportedly fell 12 percent after its quarterly results, because AI costs pushed its cash flow into the red; Intel fell almost 29 percent after a weak outlook.
- **The view:** some compare it to the dot-com bubble, others see AI as unfinished but here to stay. All figures come from X summaries and were not checked against the originals.

## Surprise of the day: AI shows climbers where to put their feet

- According to an X news summary, the Japanese creator thebigflatnow shared a concept video: glasses with AI and extended reality (XR) mark safe footholds in blue and loose rocks in red while climbing.
