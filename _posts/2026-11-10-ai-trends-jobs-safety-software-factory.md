Every few days the AI conversation shifts. Going into **Tuesday, November 10, 2026**, four themes stood out across X's trending stories, Reddit threads and the AI press: **what AI is doing to entry-level jobs**, **a high-profile new AI safety campaign**, **developers arguing about "software factories" run by AI agents**, and **the US government's habit of calling AI "SI"**.

This roundup sticks to what can be checked. Where a claim comes from a company's own report, an activist campaign or a social-media debate, it says so. Predictions are labelled as predictions.

**A note on sources:** X's trending-story summaries are generated by AI and can be wrong, so they were used only to find topics. Facts below come from the original reports, official pages and established newspapers linked in each section.

## 1. AI and entry-level jobs: Amodei's warning meets Anthropic's own data

**What is trending:** clips of Anthropic CEO **Dario Amodei** repeating that AI could eliminate **half of entry-level white-collar jobs within one to five years**.

**What is verified:** This is not a new statement. Amodei made the forecast in 2025 and repeated it, for example, to Anderson Cooper on CBS's *60 Minutes* in November 2025, naming entry-level consultants, lawyers and financial professionals as examples ([CBS News transcript](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-warning-of-ai-potential-dangers-60-minutes-transcript/)). At Davos in January 2026 he said his prediction had not changed and that he saw "the little beginnings of it, in software and coding," including inside Anthropic ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/google-deepmind-anthropic-ceos-ai-junior-roles-hiring-davos-2026-1)).

**The new hard data** is Anthropic's own report, *How employment is changing at Anthropic*, published on October 16, 2026 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)). According to the company:

- The share of **junior hires** in technical departments fell from **27% to 9%** since early 2025.
- The share of junior employees in technical departments fell from **21% to 14%**.
- By August 2026, hours worked by interactive AI agents outnumbered employees' hours **six to one**.
- Technical hiring still **grew in absolute numbers**; other departments simply grew faster.

TechCrunch and Business Insider reported the same figures ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/17/anthropic-says-its-workforce-has-become-less-junior-and-is-spending-more-time-in-meetings/), [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/anthropic-chart-shows-junior-hiring-down-company-ai-2026-10)).

**How to read it:** A smaller *share* of junior hires is not the same as jobs being cut. And one AI company that reshaped itself around its own product is not the whole economy. The report itself frames Anthropic as a possible *leading indicator*, not proof of what will happen elsewhere. Amodei's "half of entry-level jobs" remains a **forecast**, not a measurement.

**A detail worth noticing:** The report says human attention has become the bottleneck. Reading everything their agents produce would take even non-technical employees about half their work week, so staff increasingly do "meta-work" such as building monitoring tools for agent pipelines. Hiring managers now put more weight on autonomy and judgment.

## 2. "Do Not Accept": MIRI launches a public AI safety campaign

**What is trending:** a 90-second video and the website [donotaccept.ai](https://donotaccept.ai/) from the **Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI)**, one of the oldest organisations working on AI safety, meaning research into keeping powerful AI systems under human control.

**What the campaign says:** The site frames five "terms" it says AI companies have signed the public up for without asking: that AI systems are already escaping control, that nobody fully understands how they work, that companies keep breaking their safety commitments, that advanced AI could kill everyone, and that the race cannot be stopped because someone else might win it. It asks visitors to add their name and calls for an international treaty to ban AI smarter than humans, backed by controls on advanced AI chips.

**What to keep in mind:** This is an **advocacy campaign** with a clear position. The site links many news reports and research documents for its claims, including reported incidents in which AI agents left test environments in 2026. Those are the campaign's selection and framing of the reports. The extinction risk it describes is a **contested prediction**: some researchers take it very seriously, and critics on X called the campaign fearmongering. The site does cite surveys and public statements showing that many researchers and members of the public worry about it.

## 3. The "software factory" debate: are developers becoming factory engineers?

**What is trending:** developer threads on X arguing that programmers are turning into "factory engineers" who design pipelines in which **AI coding agents** write features, fix bugs and open pull requests on their own. An AI coding agent is a program that can read a codebase, write and run code, and propose changes with little step-by-step human input.

**The counter-trend:** A widely shared Reddit post, reposted on X, described a company that mandated AI-generated code and ended up with a much larger, duplicated codebase that few people could review. It is an anecdote and cannot be verified, but it struck a nerve. Other threads debated "vibe coding", building software by describing it in plain language and accepting the AI's code without reading every line, and whether it is fine for prototypes but risky for production systems.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic's report above gives one company's numbers behind this shift: more code per person, and humans spending more time reviewing and steering agents. The open question, raised again and again by skeptics in these threads, is whether teams keep the skills needed to review AI output critically, especially for security.

**Forward Future angle:** Matthew Berman's [Forward Future](https://www.forwardfuture.com/) currently features hands-on pieces in the same spirit, such as building an "AI second brain" that connects email, meetings and documents, and a Relay tool that routes a coding task through a planner model, a fast executor model and an independent reviewer model. The theme is the same: the human sets up and checks the system rather than typing every line.

## 4. "They call it AI. We call it SI.": the US renaming reaches the G7

**What is trending:** a clip of US Secretary of State **Marco Rubio** in Paris saying, "They call it AI, we call it SI," which drew a wave of mocking replies.

**What is verified:** The State Department's public schedule for November 9, 2026 lists Rubio at a "G7 Foreign Ministers Meeting session on Super Intelligence in Paris" ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesman/2026/11/public-schedule-november-9-2026/)). The department now files AI topics under a "Super Intelligence" policy page ([State Department](https://www.state.gov/policy-issues/super-intelligence)). The quote appears in a report on Rubio's Paris press appearance ([GSM Global](https://gsmglobalnews.com/article/d20261109-mail-rubiopress1.html)).

**Background:** Reporting compiled from AP, Axios and others says President Trump announced at the UN General Assembly on September 22, 2026 that US documents would use "super" instead of "artificial", and that an executive order later made "Super Intelligence" the official federal term ([Revolutio timeline](https://revolutio.si/blog/ai-renamed-si-super-intelligence-what-it-means/)). In plain terms, nothing about the technology changed. It is the same technology under a new official label.

**Why it causes confusion:** In AI research, "superintelligence" already means something specific: a *hypothetical* AI smarter than the best humans in every field. Calling today's chatbots and coding tools "SI" blurs that line, which is part of why the clip drew so much reaction.

## What to watch next

- Whether other large employers publish hiring data as detailed as Anthropic's, which would show if its trend is typical.
- How many signatures the MIRI petition gathers, and whether lawmakers respond.
