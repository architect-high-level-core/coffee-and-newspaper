Product Hunt is still one of the best-known places to launch a new tech product, but in 2026 it is crowded with AI tools. Here is how a launch works today, what the rules say, what changed this year and what practical lessons founders share. After that, three short AI news items and a surprise about lava lamps.

**Note on sources:** This overview is based mainly on Product Hunt's official help pages and launch guides, plus two launch guides by other sites, Reddit and Hacker News posts, and the Forward Future newsletter. Numbers from users (for example rankings) are their own claims and were not checked.

## 1. How a Product Hunt launch works

Product Hunt is a website where new tech products are presented every day: apps, websites, AI tools and games. The community votes with upvotes and discusses in the comments. According to Product Hunt's advertising page, 70% of its users are founders, managers, developers or data people, so the audience is mostly tech and start-up people.

Every day is its own race. Each launch goes live at 12:01 a.m. Pacific Time (normally 09:01 in Central Europe) and gets a full 24 hours. You only pick the day, up to 30 days in advance. A launch page has a name, a tagline of at most 60 characters, a short description, images or GIFs, a short demo video and a first comment from the maker telling the story behind the product. The editors decide which launches are "featured" on the front page, judging whether a product is useful, new, well made and creative. Votes alone don't decide that. Product Hunt also says you can and should post your own product; you don't need a famous "hunter".

**Original article:** <https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/17350772-launch-guide>

## 2. The rules: free to launch, never ask for upvotes

A launch is free. Product Hunt sells ads separately and says ads are kept apart from the rankings. The most important rule: don't ask for upvotes. That can get a launch disqualified, and so can mass messages, bought votes, bots, giveaways in exchange for votes and AI-generated comments. Sharing your launch and asking for feedback and comments is allowed and encouraged.

Product Hunt says it actively filters fake activity such as bot accounts, fake profiles and AI-driven actions. If points disappear, they didn't come from real users.

**Original article:** <https://www.producthunt.com/launch/sharing-your-launch>

## 3. What changed in 2026, and the AI flood

On March 27, 2026, Product Hunt tested a "Randomized Leaderboard Day": every 30 minutes the ranking was shuffled for 25 minutes, points were hidden and votes in that window counted double, so that more products got seen. Product Hunt has also shown a visual launch editor that pulls text and images from your website automatically, and the leaderboard now shows points (upvotes plus real engagement like comments) instead of plain upvotes.

The downside: many Reddit users complain that Product Hunt is flooded with look-alike AI products. One Hacker News user says his analysis of a public dataset shows launches nearly doubled in the first half of 2026, while upvotes and comments per launch almost halved. That is one person's analysis and not independently checked, but it matches the general mood.

**Original article:** <https://www.producthunt.com/changes>

## 4. Practical launch lessons founders share

- **Start early.** Good launches begin weeks ahead: create a personal account under your real name and comment on other products.
- **Set a real goal**, such as a number of new users or honest feedback, instead of chasing a ranking.
- **Make it usable right away.** Product Hunt does not feature waitlists without instant access, or products that aren't live yet.
- **Explain it simply.** Product Hunt calls it the "Mom Test": would your mother understand the one-sentence pitch?
- **Show it in action** with screenshots and a short demo video, and tell your honest story in the maker comment.
- **Be there all day.** The US West Coast wakes up around lunchtime in Europe. Product Hunt notes that an earlier start can help European start-ups, but a different time only works by emailing them.
- **Know the risks.** You may not be featured, the traffic spike is usually short, and on launch day people often message you to sell upvotes. Ignore them; bought votes can be removed and hurt the launch.

## 5. Anthropic launches Claude Duet, and 1Password lets agents log in without seeing passwords

According to Forward Future, Anthropic has launched Claude Duet, an app with a proactive AI agent for professionals and small businesses. It watches connected workspaces, writes summaries and handles tasks in the background. It is a waitlist beta, at first only for US customers on Claude Pro or Max, on iOS, Android and the web. Before sensitive actions such as payments, a person has to approve.

In the same newsletter: "1Password for Agents" lets AI assistants ask permission to use a saved password. 1Password then fills it in directly on the website, without the agent ever seeing the password itself.

**Original article:** <https://forwardfuture.com/newsletter/daily/2026-10-23/ai-agents-map-brain-disease-hackers-weaponize-ai-meta-s-stock-plan>

## 6. Nvidia builds an AI data centre in a shipping container

Nvidia presented "DSX POD", a blueprint for a complete computing system that partners assemble and test inside a container before shipping it. According to Nvidia, this cuts the time to set up an AI data centre from about six months to as little as 14 days. Nvidia also introduced "OpenShell", a free system that locks in and monitors AI agents while they use software and data, which fits this week's news about agents switching off their own monitors.

**Original article:** <https://forwardfuture.com/newsletter/daily/2026-10-22/spacex-s-ai-boom-openai-safety-failures-critical-php-flaws>

## Surprise: lava lamps help protect the internet

Cloudflare films a wall full of lava lamps and turns the images of the moving blobs into data. Because the patterns are completely unpredictable, they provide real randomness, and randomness is a key ingredient of encryption. The data is mixed with other random sources and helps secure internet connections. So the lamps are more than decoration.
